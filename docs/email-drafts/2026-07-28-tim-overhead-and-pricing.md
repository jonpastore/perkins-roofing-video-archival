# 2026-07-28 — Tim / Marco / Josh: overhead in the price, and a pricing question

Subject: Your overhead number vs. what the jobs are actually recovering

---

Tim,

Short one. Three things came out of wiring your overhead into the estimator, and one of them
needs your call before I set the number.

**1. The daily overhead we quote should be one number per branch.**

You said it on the call: monthly fixed overhead ÷ 20 work days. Jupiter $28k → $1,400/day,
Miami $85k → $4,250/day. The estimator picks the days, overhead is days × that number, and the
margin slider is the only thing that moves in a negotiation. I'm building it that way.

**2. At $1,400/day, your last 21 jobs show almost no margin.**

I priced 21 of the homes you sent using your own day counts and your $1,400/day. Against what you
actually charged, the median job comes out with **‑0.4% margin**, and 19 of the 21 land below your
own $2,500-a-week minimum.

Read the other way: your prices are recovering about **$840–950 a day** of overhead, not $1,400.
That's close to the per-day rates you emailed on 7/24 ($745 tile, $700 shingle, $850 metal,
$1,050 demo) — those match your prices well. The $1,400 is the number that doesn't.

So either the jobs are carrying less overhead than the branch actually costs, or the $1,400
includes costs you don't expect a single job to cover. Which is it?

**3. Two other things from your own numbers point the same way.**

From the samples we have: materials are up about **8% year over year** (TU Plus $105.20 → $113.90,
MTS $105.20 → $114.25, XFR $123.80 → $132.25, April 2026 list), while the jobs we've seen are down
about **24% from the 2024 peak** (tile $1,448 → $1,100/sq, metal $1,705 → $1,276/sq).

Costs up, prices down, and overhead under-recovered. That's three separate measurements saying the
same thing, all from your own paperwork.

**What repricing at $1,400 would do:** the overhead line on a typical job roughly doubles, and
quotes come out about **13% higher** than what you've been charging. That may be exactly the
correction you want — but it's your call, not something I'll switch on quietly.

**The question I need answered:**

Should we store the **monthly** overhead number and divide by the actual working days in that month
— Monday to Friday, less national holidays — instead of a flat 20?

Worth knowing before you decide: 2026 has **250 working days**, which averages **20.8 a month**,
not 22. So your ÷20 is only about **4% conservative**, not 10%. On an 8-day Jupiter job that's
$11,200 of overhead instead of $10,752 — $448 of cushion, not the bigger buffer it might look like.

If the intent is to build in negotiating room, ÷20 barely does it, and it's worth deciding
deliberately how much room you want rather than getting it as a rounding artifact.

Jon
